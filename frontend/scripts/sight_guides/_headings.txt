Recognized for its crisp, temperate alpine air and cool breezes, French administrators developed the summit as a high-altitude sanatorium and luxury summer retreat (station d'altitude) to escape the sweltering heat and tropical diseases of the coastal lowlands. During the early 20th century, over 200 colonial villas, sports clubs, hotels, and paved roads were constructed across the ridge. Following decades of war and abandonment during the mid-20th century, the site was comprehensively redeveloped by the Sun Group starting in 2007, transforming the former colonial hill station into one of Southeast Asia's premier mountain theme parks and resorts.
